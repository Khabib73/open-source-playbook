# Asking to be assigned

Many projects use labels and issue tracking to manage who works on what. When you want to take on an issue, you usually ask a maintainer to assign it to you. This is a normal and welcome request — projects love people who want to contribute. The key is to ask politely, not to demand.

Being assigned is a small commitment. It tells the project "I will work on this". So only ask when you actually plan to do the work.

## Request, don't demand

The most common mistake is wording the request like a command. Remember, the maintainer is a person with many tasks. A polite request is much more likely to succeed.

> **Bad:** "You must assign this issue to me."
>
> **Good:** "Could you please assign this issue to me? I'd like to work on it."

## Show your intention

Maintainers feel safer assigning work to someone who seems serious. If you can, mention what you plan to do, or confirm that you understand the task. This builds trust.

> **Good:** "I'd like to work on this issue. I've read the related code and I think I can fix it. Could you assign it to me?"

## Be patient

Sometimes the maintainer is busy, or maybe someone already claimed the issue. If you do not get an answer right away, it is fine to wait a bit or ask again politely later. Do not insist or push.

> **Good:** "Just checking in — is this issue still available? I'm still happy to take it on."

## Respect ownership

If an issue is already assigned to someone else, do not ask to take it over. Offer to help instead, or wait until the issue becomes available again.

> **Good:** "I see this is assigned to someone else. If you ever need an extra pair of hands, I'm happy to help."

## Ready-to-use phrases

> "Could you please assign this issue to me?"
>
> "I'd like to work on this — may I take it?"
>
> "If no one else is working on it, I'd be happy to pick it up."

## A simple rule

Treat assignment as a polite request between collaborators, not a demand. Show that you are serious, and respect the other person's time and decisions. That is how you earn trust and get more opportunities to contribute.
